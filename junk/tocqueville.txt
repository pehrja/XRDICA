Amongst the novel objects that attracted
my attention during my stay in the
United States, nothing struck me more
forcibly than the general equality of conditions.
I readily discovered the prodigious influence
which this primary fact exercises on
the whole course of society, by giving
a certain direction to public opinion,
and a certain tenor to the laws.
I speedily perceived that the influence
of this fact extends far beyond the
political character and the laws of the country,
and that it has no less empire over
civil society than over the Government.
